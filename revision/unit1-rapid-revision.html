01 Scope & the fluid-mosaic membrane

Biochemistry = chemistry of life processes. In veterinary practice it supplies the diagnostic (serum enzymes, BUN), nutritional (limiting amino acid, EFA), therapeutic (fluid therapy, PGF2α) and toxicological (xenobiotic, P450) framework.

Fluid mosaic model — Singer & Nicolson, 1972. A fluid lipid bilayer in which globular proteins float like icebergs. Bilayer = amphipathic phospholipids (polar head out, acyl tails in) + cholesterol + glycolipids. Proteins are integral/intrinsic (span bilayer, need detergent, e.g. Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase, glycophorin) or peripheral/extrinsic (surface, salt extractable, e.g. spectrin, cyt c).

7–10nm thickness
18%protein — myelin (lowest)
76%protein — inner mito (highest)

  • Asymmetry: outer leaflet = phosphatidylcholine, sphingomyelin, all glycolipids & glycoproteins; inner leaflet = phosphatidylethanolamine, phosphatidylserine, PIP2. PS flipped outward = "eat-me" apoptosis signal (basis of Annexin-V assay).
  • Movement of lipids: lateral diffusion & flexion are fast; transverse flip-flop is very slow and needs enzymes — flippase (inward, ATP), floppase (outward, ATP), scramblase (both ways, Ca²⁺-activated, no ATP).
  • Fluidity ↑ by unsaturated & short-chain fatty acids and ↑ temperature; ↓ by saturated/long chains. Cholesterol is a fluidity buffer — below transition temp it prevents crystalline packing (↑fluidity); above it restrains chains (↓fluidity).
  • Functions: selective permeability, compartmentation, electrical excitability, receptor & signal transduction, cell recognition (glycocalyx), antigenicity (blood groups), cell adhesion, energy transduction (mitochondrial inner membrane).
Trap: the bilayer is freely permeable to small uncharged molecules (O₂, CO₂, N₂, urea, ethanol, steroids). It is essentially impermeable to ions and to glucose — even Na⁺ and K⁺, being small, are blocked because of their large hydration shells.

02 Transport across membranes — the master map

Uniport one solute, one way — GLUT1 Symport two solutes, same way — SGLT-1 Antiport two solutes, opposite — Cl⁻/HCO₃⁻ Bulk endo/exo/pinocytosis, phagocytosis Ionophores valinomycin (K⁺ carrier) · gramicidin (channel)
Most-asked: Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase — electrogenic (net +1 out), consumes ≈30 % of basal ATP, inhibited by ouabain / digitalis / cardiac glycosides and by K⁺-free medium; it creates the Na⁺ gradient that powers all secondary active transport, and maintains cell volume against Donnan swelling.

04 Gibbs–Donnan membrane equilibrium

Statement. When a non-diffusible charged ion (plasma protein anion, Pr⁻) is confined to one side of a semipermeable membrane, the diffusible ions redistribute unequally so that the product of diffusible ion concentrations is equal on both sides, while electroneutrality holds on each side.

[Na⁺]i × [Cl⁻]i = [Na⁺]o × [Cl⁻]o  ·  Donnan ratio r = [Na⁺]i/[Na⁺]o = [Cl⁻]o/[Cl⁻]i ≈ 0.95
  • Three consequences: ① the protein side holds more diffusible cations, fewer anions; ② total osmotically active particles are higher on the protein side → water pulled in (Donnan effect adds ≈ 1/3 extra to colloid osmotic pressure); ③ a Donnan potential develops across the membrane.
  • Veterinary significance: plasma vs interstitial fluid ion split; contribution to resting membrane potential; the chloride shift (Hamburger phenomenon) in RBC; oedema of hypoproteinaemia (parasitism, PLE, liver failure) — falling albumin collapses both oncotic and Donnan pressure; behaviour of colloids (dextran, plasma) in fluid therapy; ultrafiltration in the glomerulus.
  • Double Donnan / pump-leak: if Donnan forces acted alone the cell would swell and burst. Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase keeps Na⁺ functionally impermeant outside, opposing it — cell volume is a steady state, not equilibrium.

05 Dissociation of acids · pH · buffers · Henderson–Hasselbalch

Brønsted–Lowry: acid = proton donor, base = proton acceptor. Strength of a weak acid is its pKa = pH at which it is exactly half dissociated. Lower pKa = stronger acid. pH = −log[H⁺]; blood [H⁺] ≈ 40 nmol/L.

Buffer = weak acid + its conjugate base; resists pH change on adding acid/alkali. Buffer capacity is maximal at pH = pKa and useful only within pKa ± 1 — this is why the flat middle of a titration curve is the buffering zone.

Henderson–Hasselbalch  pH = pKa + log ([salt]/[acid]) = pKa + log ([A⁻]/[HA])
For blood: pH = 6.1 + log (HCO₃⁻ / 0.03 × pCO₂) = 6.1 + log (24/1.2) = 6.1 + log 20 = 7.4
The 20 : 1 ratio is the single most examined number in the unit. pH depends on the ratio, not absolute values — which is why compensation (renal HCO₃⁻ retention, respiratory CO₂ blow-off) restores pH by restoring the ratio.

06 Definition, classification & isomerism

Carbohydrates = polyhydroxy aldehydes or ketones, or substances yielding them on hydrolysis; empirical formula (CH₂O)n. Classified as monosaccharides (cannot be hydrolysed), disaccharides, oligosaccharides (2–10), polysaccharides (homo- vs hetero-).

Aldose −CHO (glucose) Ketose C=O (fructose) Epimers differ at ONE C: glucose↔galactose at C-4, glucose↔mannose at C-2 Anomers α/β at C-1 (anomeric carbon) Enantiomers D/L — mirror at penultimate C; body sugars are D Mutarotation α +112° → +52.7° → β +18.7°
  • Reducing sugars have a free anomeric −OH → reduce Benedict's/Fehling's. All monosaccharides, maltose, isomaltose, lactose, cellobiose are reducing. Sucrose and trehalose are non-reducing (both anomeric carbons locked).
  • Ring forms: pyranose (6-membered) & furanose (5-membered); Haworth projection; chair form of β-D-glucopyranose is the most stable sugar because all bulky groups are equatorial.
  • Derivatives: sugar acids (gluconic, glucuronic — conjugation & detoxification, ascorbic acid), sugar alcohols (sorbitol → cataract in diabetes, glycerol, inositol), deoxy sugars (deoxyribose, fucose), amino sugars (glucosamine, galactosamine, N-acetylneuraminic acid = sialic acid), sugar esters (glucose-6-P).

10 Mucopolysaccharides (GAGs) & bacterial cell-wall polysaccharides

GAGs = long unbranched heteropolysaccharides of a repeating disaccharide = uronic acid (or galactose) + amino sugar, almost all sulphated and strongly anionic. Bound to a core protein they form proteoglycans (ground substance) — a huge hydrated gel that resists compression, lubricates and traps cations and water.

Bacterial cell wall — peptidoglycan (murein). Alternating N-acetylglucosamine (NAG) and N-acetylmuramic acid (NAM) joined β1→4; NAM carries a tetrapeptide, and chains are cross-linked by transpeptidase (pentaglycine bridge in Staphylococcus).

NAG–NAM β1→4→tetrapeptide→transpeptidase cross-link→rigid sacculus
  • Lysozyme (tears, saliva, egg white, phagocytes) hydrolyses the β1→4 NAM–NAG bond → lysis. First-line innate defence.
  • Penicillins & cephalosporins inhibit transpeptidase (PBP) → wall cannot cross-link. Selective toxicity rests on the fact that the host has no peptidoglycan.
  • Gram-positive: thick peptidoglycan + teichoic acid (glycerol/ribitol phosphate — adhesion, antigenicity).
  • Gram-negative: thin peptidoglycan + outer membrane bearing lipopolysaccharide (LPS): O-antigen + core + lipid A = the endotoxin — the trigger of coliform mastitis, endotoxic shock and DIC (ties directly into Unit 3 "oxidative stress & shock").
  • Capsular polysaccharides resist phagocytosis and are the basis of serotyping and of many vaccines.

11 Bloor's classification — the skeleton of every lipid answer

Common properties: insoluble in water, soluble in organic solvents (ether, chloroform, benzene); amphipathic if a polar head exists; highest calorific value 9.3 kcal/g (vs 4.1 for carbohydrate/protein); non-polar so stored anhydrous — the most economical fuel store. Functions: concentrated energy, structural (membranes, myelin), thermal insulation & padding, carrier of vitamins ADEK, precursor of steroid hormones & eicosanoids, source of essential fatty acids, surfactant, milk-fat and egg-yolk quality.

12 Fatty acids & essential fatty acids

  • Saturated: butyric C4 (rumen VFA, milk fat), palmitic C16, stearic C18. Unsaturated: oleic 18:1 ω9, linoleic 18:2 ω6, α-linolenic 18:3 ω3, arachidonic 20:4 ω6, EPA 20:5, DHA 22:6.
  • Essential fatty acids = linoleic + α-linolenic — mammals lack Δ12 and Δ15 desaturase so cannot insert double bonds beyond C-9. Arachidonic acid is semi-essential (made from linoleic).
  • EFA deficiency: scaly dermatitis, dull dry coat, poor wound healing, impaired reproduction, fatty liver, ↑ transepidermal water loss.
  • Cat exception: the cat has negligible Δ6-desaturase → arachidonic acid is dietary-essential for cats, which is why cats are obligate carnivores nutritionally.
  • Natural cis configuration bends the chain (↑fluidity, ↓melting point); industrial trans behaves like saturated.
Trap: "ω" numbering counts from the methyl end, "Δ" from the carboxyl end. Arachidonic acid = 20:4 Δ5,8,11,14 = ω6.

16 Prostaglandins & the eicosanoid tree

Eicosanoids = 20-carbon (eicosa) derivatives of arachidonic acid, released from membrane phospholipid by phospholipase A₂. They are local hormones (autacoids) — made on demand, act on the cell of origin or its neighbour, and are destroyed within seconds; they are not stored. Basic skeleton = prostanoic acid (cyclopentane ring + two side chains).

18 Four levels of structure

Primary
Linear amino-acid sequence + disulphide positions. Bond: peptide (covalent). Determines all higher orders. First sequenced protein = insulin (Sanger). A single substitution can be lethal (Hb-S).
Secondary
Local regular folding held by hydrogen bonds between backbone C=O and N–H. α-helix: right-handed, 3.6 residues per turn, pitch 5.4 Å, H-bond n→n+4; broken by proline ("helix breaker") & charged clusters. β-pleated sheet: parallel/antiparallel, H-bonds between strands (silk fibroin, keratin). Collagen triple helix: left-handed strands wound right, repeating Gly–X–Y (X often proline, Y hydroxyproline); glycine at every third residue is obligatory.
Tertiary
Overall 3-D shape of one chain; stabilised chiefly by hydrophobic interactions, plus ionic/salt bridges, H-bonds, van der Waals and disulphide bonds (the only covalent one). Creates the active site / binding cleft. Example: myoglobin.
Quaternary
Association of ≥2 subunits by non-covalent forces. Haemoglobin α₂β₂, LDH, aldolase, immunoglobulins. Basis of co-operativity and allosteric regulation (sigmoid O₂ curve of Hb vs hyperbolic curve of myoglobin).
Vitamin C link: ascorbate is the cofactor for prolyl and lysyl hydroxylase. No vitamin C → no hydroxyproline → collagen triple helix cannot cross-link → scurvy (guinea pig, primates, fish — species that lack L-gulonolactone oxidase). Poor wound healing + capillary fragility.

19 Properties of proteins

  • Amphoteric & isoelectric point (pI): net charge zero at pI → minimum solubility, maximum precipitation, no movement in an electric field. Casein pI = 4.6 — the reason milk curdles when it sours or when acid/rennet is added, and the basis of paneer/dahi. Serum albumin pI ≈ 4.9.
  • Electrophoresis: at pH 8.6 all plasma proteins are anionic and separate as albumin → α₁ → α₂ → β → γ. This is the direct link to dysproteinaemias in Unit 3.
  • Denaturation: heat, extremes of pH, urea, guanidine, heavy metals, organic solvents, UV, vigorous shaking. Secondary, tertiary and quaternary structure are destroyed; the peptide bonds of the primary structure remain intact. Result: loss of biological activity, ↓solubility, ↑viscosity, ↑digestibility, exposure of −SH groups. Usually irreversible (boiled egg) but sometimes reversible (ribonuclease).
  • Precipitation: by heavy-metal ions (Hg²⁺, Pb²⁺ — protein/egg white is a first-aid antidote), by alkaloidal reagents (tungstic, trichloroacetic, picric, sulphosalicylic acid — used for deproteinising blood before glucose estimation), by organic solvents, and by salting out — globulin at half saturation and albumin at full saturation with ammonium sulphate.
  • Colloidal nature: large size, Tyndall effect, non-dialysable, exert osmotic (oncotic) pressure ≈ 25 mmHg.

21 Physical & chemical properties of amino acids · the peptide bond

Amphoteric nature & zwitterion. In solution an amino acid exists as a dipolar ion with −NH₃⁺ and −COO⁻. It therefore acts as an acid and a base, and is a good buffer. Hence high melting points, water solubility, and large dipole moments — they behave like salts, not organic solids.

Isoelectric pH: for a neutral amino acid pI = (pK₁ + pK₂)/2; for acidic and basic amino acids, average the two pK values that flank the zwitterion. Solubility and buffering are minimum at pI.

Optical activity. The α-carbon is asymmetric (except glycine) → D and L forms, dextro (+) or laevo (−) rotation. Proteins contain only L-amino acids; D-forms occur in bacterial cell walls (D-alanine, D-glutamate) and some antibiotics — which is why they resist host proteases.

Peptide bond formation. The −COOH of one amino acid condenses with the −NH₂ of the next with loss of water, giving a substituted amide (−CO−NH−) link. Chains are written N-terminal → C-terminal.

  • Has partial double-bond character from resonance → the six atoms of the bond are rigid and planar; no free rotation about the C−N bond.
  • Almost always trans (except X–proline); rotation is possible only about the φ (phi) and ψ (psi) bonds of the α-carbon — the basis of the Ramachandran plot.
  • Uncharged but polar; the C=O and N–H are exactly the H-bond donors/acceptors that build the α-helix and β-sheet.
  • Hydrolysed by strong acid/alkali or by proteolytic enzymes; two or more peptide bonds give the biuret reaction.
Trap: Biuret is negative for free amino acids and for dipeptides — it needs at least two peptide bonds (i.e. a tripeptide onwards). Ninhydrin, in contrast, is positive for free α-amino acids.

22 Bases → nucleoside → nucleotide

Purines
Fused 6+5 ring. Adenine, Guanine; also hypoxanthine, xanthine, uric acid (end product in birds & reptiles = uricotelic; in Dalmatian dogs), and methylxanthines caffeine & theobromine — theobromine of chocolate is the classic canine toxicosis.
Pyrimidines
Single 6-ring. Cytosine (both), Thymine (DNA only), Uracil (RNA only); orotic acid is the pathway intermediate. Mnemonic: CUT the PY — Cytosine, Uracil, Thymine are PYrimidines.
Naming
Adenine→adenosine→AMP/ADP/ATP; Guanine→guanosine→GMP; Cytosine→cytidine; Uracil→uridine; Thymine→thymidine. Add "deoxy" for the DNA series.
Biological significance of nucleotides (a guaranteed short note): ① energy currency — ATP, GTP, and the high-energy phosphoanhydride bond; ② second messengers — cAMP, cGMP; ③ coenzymes — NAD⁺, NADP⁺, FAD, CoA, all built on an adenine nucleotide; ④ activated carriers — UDP-glucose (glycogen), CDP-choline (phospholipid), SAM (methyl), PAPS (sulphate, for GAGs & detoxification); ⑤ monomers of DNA & RNA; ⑥ allosteric regulators (ATP/AMP ratio); ⑦ drug targets — allopurinol, 5-fluorouracil, azathioprine.

23 DNA — Watson & Crick, 1953

  • Two antiparallel polynucleotide chains (one 5′→3′, the other 3′→5′) wound into a right-handed double helix; sugar-phosphate backbone outside joined by 3′,5′-phosphodiester bonds, bases stacked inside.
  • Complementary base pairing: A = T (2 hydrogen bonds), G ≡ C (3 hydrogen bonds) — always a purine with a pyrimidine, keeping the diameter constant.
  • Chargaff's rules: A = T, G = C, therefore purines = pyrimidines; the (A+T)/(G+C) ratio is species-specific.
  • Major and minor grooves — the binding sites of regulatory proteins and many drugs.

  • Forms: B-DNA = physiological, right-handed; A-DNA = dehydrated, right-handed; Z-DNA = left-handed, zig-zag, alternating purine-pyrimidine (GC repeats).
  • Denaturation / melting: heat or alkali separates the strands. Tm rises with G+C content (3 H-bonds). Strand separation exposes the bases → hyperchromic effect: absorbance at 260 nm increases. Slow cooling → renaturation/annealing (the principle of hybridisation and PCR).
  • Packaging: DNA + histones (H2A, H2B, H3, H4 octamer) wrapped by ~146 bp = nucleosome, sealed by H1 → solenoid → chromatin → chromosome. Supercoiling is managed by topoisomerases/gyrase (target of fluoroquinolones in bacteria).
  • Functions: repository of genetic information, replication (semi-conservative), transcription, mutation & variation, molecular diagnosis (PCR), parentage & breed identification.

24 RNA — a typical ribonucleic acid

Single-stranded, ribose sugar, uracil in place of thymine, alkali-labile (2′-OH), can fold on itself into local double-helical hairpins.
